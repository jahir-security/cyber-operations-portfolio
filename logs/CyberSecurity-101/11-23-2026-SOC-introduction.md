# SOC Introduction - [11-23-2026]

## What This Covers

This lab introduces the fundamentals of a Security Operations Center (SOC) and explains how organizations continuously monitor their networks, systems, and resources to detect and respond to security threats. It covers the three main pillars of a SOC: People, Process, and Technology, along with the responsibilities of different SOC team members, including Level 1, Level 2, and Level 3 SOC Analysts, Security Engineers, Detection Engineers, and SOC Managers. The lab explores alert triage using the 5 Ws (What, When, Where, Who, and Why), incident reporting, incident response, and digital forensics. It also introduces important security technologies such as SIEM, EDR, and Firewalls. Finally, I worked through a practical SOC exercise in which I had to act as a Level 1 Analyst, investigate a port-scanning alert using the provided SIEM logs, identify the relevant details of the activity, determine whether it was intended or malicious, and examine whether any response had been sent to the scanning host.

## Key Concepts

* Understanding the purpose of a Security Operations Center (SOC) and its role in protecting organizational networks and systems.
* Understanding continuous security monitoring and the importance of detecting and responding to threats 24/7.
* Understanding the two primary responsibilities of a SOC: Detection and Response.
* Identifying vulnerabilities in operating systems, applications, and network devices that could be exploited by attackers.
* Detecting unauthorized activity, including suspicious account logins and unusual geographic login locations.
* Understanding security policy violations and how activities outside organizational policies can create security risks.
* Understanding network intrusions and unauthorized access to systems and applications.
* Understanding incident response and how it helps minimize the impact of security incidents and identify their root causes.
* Understanding the three pillars of a SOC: People, Process, and Technology.
* Understanding the responsibilities of a Level 1 SOC Analyst, including initial alert triage and reporting.
* Understanding the responsibilities of a Level 2 SOC Analyst, including deeper investigation and correlation of information from multiple data sources.
* Understanding the responsibilities of a Level 3 SOC Analyst, including threat hunting and supporting containment, eradication, and recovery.
* Understanding the role of a Security Engineer in deploying and configuring security solutions.
* Understanding the role of a Detection Engineer in developing and maintaining detection rules.
* Understanding the role of a SOC Manager in managing SOC processes and communicating with the organization's CISO.
* Understanding alert triage and how analysts determine the severity and priority of security alerts.
* Applying the 5 Ws during alert investigation:
  * What - What activity triggered the alert?
  * When - When did the activity occur?
  * Where - Where did the activity take place?
  * Who - Who was involved in the activity?
  * Why - Why did the activity occur?
* Understanding the importance of documenting investigation findings and escalating harmful alerts through tickets and reports.
* Understanding how screenshots and supporting evidence improve the quality of incident reports.
* Understanding incident response and digital forensics, including identifying the root cause through system and network artifacts.
* Understanding SIEM (Security Information and Event Management) and how it collects and correlates logs from multiple sources to generate security alerts.
* Understanding EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response) and its ability to provide real-time and historical endpoint visibility and automated response capabilities.
* Understanding Firewalls and how they monitor and filter incoming and outgoing network traffic.
* Recognising other SOC technologies, including Antivirus, EPP, IDS/IPS, XDR, and SOAR.
* Applying the 5 Ws to investigate a malware detection scenario and a network port-scanning alert.
* Understanding how prior notification from a vulnerability assessment team can help determine whether detected scanning activity is authorized or suspicious.
